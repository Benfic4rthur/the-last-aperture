Build a small first-person browser game called The Last Aperture. Let the player walk through a quiet submerged observatory, restore its telescope with one switch, and watch a deep-ocean panorama appear through the dome.

PRIORITIES
1. A memorable reveal and a coherent underwater atmosphere.
2. Smooth movement and clear interaction.
3. A small, self-contained build.

CONSTRAINTS
- Use raw WebGPU and procedural geometry, materials, and effects. Ship a single self-contained index.html with zero dependencies or external assets.
- Keep the scope to one room, one switch, and one ending. Omit combat, enemies, inventory, and time limits.
- Preallocate frame data and reuse GPU buffers.

TARGET
- Apple M1 Pro, Microsoft Edge, 1920×1080 output at 60 Hz. Lower internal resolution first if needed.
- Over a scripted 60-second playthrough, aim for an average GPU frame time of 16.7 ms or less and a worst GPU frame of 25 ms or less.

SCENE
- Build a circular observatory with a shallow glass dome, a central brass telescope, and a single control pedestal.
- Use deep indigo water, muted teal light, and a small amber glow on the switch to draw the player across the room.
- Keep the exterior dim at first. Fine drifting particles and dark rock silhouettes suggest depth beyond the glass.
- Make the view from behind the telescope the hero frame. On activation, rotate the telescope toward the dome and sweep a slow ribbon of blue-green light across the rocks. Let the view linger after the reveal.

CONTROLS
- Use WASD to walk, the mouse to look, E to activate the switch, Esc to pause, and R to restart.
- Show a small interaction cue only when the player can reach the switch. A soft synthesized chime confirms activation.
- The reveal completes the goal. Keep the player free to look around. A first run should take about one minute.

VERIFICATION
- Capture six GPU-rendered views in Edge: start, pedestal, inactive dome, telescope close-up, mid-reveal, and final vista. Give a fresh Codex session only the images and a short recording. Fix visible defects above minor severity.
- Measure the scripted run and report average, 99th-percentile, and maximum frame time. Use GPU timestamp queries where supported.
- Verify that a first-time player can find and activate the switch, that the reveal only fires once per run, and that restarting restores the initial state.

DONE WHEN
Deliver index.html, its size, and brief visual, performance, and playtest results.
